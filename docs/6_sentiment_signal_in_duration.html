Sentiment signal across duration Duration isn't the driver — domain is. Numbers are from the 2 s-filtered matrix and will be redone on the full-ROG matrix.

Comparing sentiment signal across clip length on ROG-Dialog (short colloquial, 2–8 s) and ParlaSpeech-SI (parliament speech, 2–30 s). The "signal climbs with duration" story on ROG turns out to be a floor effect, not a universal law — parliament speech sits at F1 ≈ 0.9 across every duration bin, dialogue climbs from 0.5 to 0.65 then stops. The ~0.3 F1 gap is domain, not length.

Speech-rate control — same conclusion from a different angle
  • Split each test source into 4 speech-rate quartiles (words / second) and re-bin inside each quartile.
  • On ParlaSpeech-SI · audio-sec: 0 / 4 quartiles show a monotone+ shape on xlm-r-parla and xlm-r-parlasent. Duration doesn't matter within-WPS.

Revised conclusion — what the duration study tells us right now.

  • No peak-and-fall.
    Parliament speech sentiment is near-flat at F1 ≈ 0.88 – 0.95 from 4.5 s to 23 s. No hidden peak above ROG's 6 s range.
  • ROG's "longer = better" is a minimum-context effect.
    Below ~3 s there's not enough lexical material to classify colloquial sentiment; above that ROG plateaus at its domain ceiling (~0.65 F1).
  • Domain beats length by ~0.3 F1.
    Same model family, overlapping length band (4 – 6 s), same ordinal scheme — parliament 0.93 vs dialogue 0.63. That's the real gap to close.
  • Simpler curation rule.
    For a dialogue training pool: ≥ 3 s (or ≥ 5 words) and a soft WPS cap around 3.0. Length beyond ~4 s does not buy more sentiment signal on dialogue.
  • Known gap in the comparison.
    ROG and ParlaSpeech-SI differ in clip length, speech rate and label prior — so the 0.3 F1 "domain gap" bundles all three. A matched-distribution ParlaSpeech test set (sampled to mimic ROG's stats) is needed before we attribute the gap to register alone.
How this was measured, and a note on ρ vs R²
  • Binning. Quantile (equal-count) bins over each test source's own distribution. ROG test n = 434 → 5 duration bins of ~87 each; ParlaSpeech-SI test n = 600 → 5 duration bins of ~120 each. Self-labelled cells (xlm-r-parlasent × any mixture containing P) excluded from the envelope.
  • ρ (Spearman) is used to classify the shape of the envelope across bins — a rank correlation of (bin-centre, F1_3) across 3 – 5 points. Answers "does F1 go up or down with duration?". Scale-free, so a small rise and a huge rise score the same. ρ ≥ +0.6 = monotone+, ≤ −0.6 = monotone−, |ρ| < 0.3 = flat.
  • R² is used on the raw regression (predicting the 0–5 sentiment scalar on each test row). Answers "how much of the gold variance did the model explain?" on the actual scale, so sign and magnitude both matter. R² = 0 means the model is as good as predicting the mean; R² < 0 means it is worse than that.
  • Why both. A model can rank test items well (high ρ) while being offset in magnitude (low or negative R²). Several S-only cells on ROG show ρ ≈ +0.3 but R² ≈ −0.6 — they roughly sort dialogue utterances by polarity but predict the wrong absolute values. One number alone would hide the other story.
