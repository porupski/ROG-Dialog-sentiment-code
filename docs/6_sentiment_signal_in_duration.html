Proportional-duration hypothesis — ruled out

Earlier working hypothesis: a 1 s dialogue turn might carry roughly the sentiment signal of a 5 s parliament turn, so the 0.3 F1 gap between domains might reduce to a duration gap. The full-ROG numbers kill this.

  • At matched duration (2–4 s or 4–8 s), the LLM is ~0.2 F1_3 behind parliament and the local fine-tune is ~0.3 behind. Duration is matched; register and lexical content differ. Gap is domain, not length.
  • The sub-1 s band on ROG doesn't exist on ParlaSpeech (parliament clips start at ~2.25 s). No parliamentary clip operates in the regime where ROG's lowest-signal utterances live, so the "1 s dialogue ↔ 5 s parliament" mapping is untestable on this side.
  • Context on Flash lifts the sub-1 s floor from 0.42 to 0.51 but cannot push it to parliament's ~0.94 at any duration. Even Flash at 4–8 s (0.77) doesn't reach parliament at 2–4 s (0.94).
  • Short dialogue isn't carrying hidden signal that long context recovers — it's missing lexical material, and context is an incomplete substitute. Verdict: the ~0.2 F1_3 at-matched-duration gap is the real floor the project has to close, not a duration artefact.
Speech-rate control — same conclusion from a different angle
  • Split each test source into 4 speech-rate quartiles (words / second) and re-bin inside each quartile.
  • ParlaSpeech-SI · audio-sec: 4 / 4 quartiles show a monotone+ shape on xlm-r-parla and xlm-r-parlasent. Duration doesn't matter within WPS.
  • ROG · audio-sec: 1 / 4 quartiles survive per backbone — partial confound.
  • WPS ranges split by domain: parliament 0.9 – 4.4 wps (prepared, slow); ROG 0.25 – 5.4 wps (conversational, wider spread).

Final read.

  • No peak-and-fall. Parliament sentiment sits at F1 ≈ 0.88–0.95 from 4 s to 23 s. No hidden peak above ROG's 8 s range.
  • Sub-2 s clips are inherently hard. Flash iso lands at 0.42 on sub-1 s vs 0.72 at 4–8 s. Context (W=40) softens the penalty by +0.09 F1_3 but doesn't close it.
  • Domain beats length by ~0.2 F1_3 at matched duration. ROG 4–8 s with the best LLM = 0.77; ParlaSpeech 4–8 s with a parliament-tuned fine-tune = 0.95. Both are 4–8 s. The 0.18 gap is register, lexical density, and label distribution.
  • Local fine-tune nearly catches the LLM at 4–8 s (0.687 vs 0.772) but collapses on sub-1 s (0.361 vs 0.505). Local models need lexical material; context can only partially substitute. See Report 4 for the broader local story.
  • Curation rule, revised. For a dialogue training pool: no filter on duration — the sub-2 s rows are the bulk of the data (81 % of test) and training on them matters. Context at target time (W ≥ 20) is the lever that pulls short-clip F1 up.
  • Known gap in the comparison. ROG and ParlaSpeech-SI differ in clip length, speech rate, lexical density, and label prior. The 0.2 F1 gap at matched duration bundles register and prior together. A matched-distribution ParlaSpeech test (sampled to mimic ROG's stats) would separate them.
How this was measured, and a note on ρ vs R²
  • Full-ROG binning. Fixed duration bands {<1, 1–2, 2–4, 4–8, ≥8 s} over the full rog_dia_full test (n=2233); each cell's predictions.jsonl joined to the corpus on instance_id for duration. Labels normalized to {negative, neutral, positive}; macro-F1 computed per band.
  • Parliament numbers (ParlaSpeech-SI rows above) come from the 2 s-filtered matrix at quantile bins; those cells weren't re-run for full-ROG because ParlaSpeech is a separate eval set unchanged by the ROG-side duration filter.
  • ρ (Spearman) classifies the shape of the envelope across bins — a rank correlation of (bin-centre, F1_3). Scale-free, so a small rise and a huge rise score the same. ρ ≥ +0.6 = monotone+, ≤ −0.6 = monotone−, |ρ| < 0.3 = flat.
  • R² is used on the raw regression (0–5 sentiment scalar). Sign and magnitude both matter. R² = 0 ≡ predicting the mean; R² < 0 ≡ worse than that.
  • Why both. A model can rank items well (high ρ) while being offset in magnitude (low R²). Several S-only cells on ROG show ρ ≈ +0.3 but R² ≈ −0.6 — correct polarity order, wrong absolute values.
